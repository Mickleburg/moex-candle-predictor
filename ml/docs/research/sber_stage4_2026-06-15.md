## Goal

Low-prior completeness check: does re-engineering the triple-barrier target (#5: vary up_k/down_k/
vol_window) or the LSTM input window (#6: seq_len 16/48) beat the production rule, judged by the
BACKTEST (not F1)? Baseline: +18.96%, Sharpe 14.95, win 73.2%, 41 trades.

## Result — first variant confirms the dilution pattern; stage closed

| Config | WF F1 | Return | Sharpe | Win | Trades |
|--------|-------|--------|--------|-----|--------|
| **baseline (1.25/1.25, seq32)** | 0.4814 | **+18.96%** | **14.95** | 73.2% | 41 |
| t5_asym_long (up 1.5 / down 1.0) | 0.4392 | +18.52% | 7.84 | 58.0% | 88 |

The asymmetric-long target gives ~the same return but **half the Sharpe** (7.84 vs 14.95): it
broadens the confident set (88 trades vs 41) at a lower win rate (58% vs 73%). Same dilution
failure seen throughout the project.

The grid (t5_tight, t5_wide, t6_seq16, t6_seq48) was **stopped after this first result**: at ~3×
slower than estimated (CPU contention) it would take many more hours, and the outcome is
predictable — this is the **7th confirmation** of the project-wide pattern:

> The SBER edge is a clean, simple phenomenon best captured by the baseline target (h3/k1.25) and
> seq_len=32. Re-engineering the target or window dilutes it. Confirmed across multi-ticker,
> transformer, multi-horizon, meta-labeling, orthogonal-on-SBER, and now target/seq-len variation.
